## Question 1: Probability & Set Theory (10 points)

a) Define the sample space for a single toss of a fair coin.

b) Let event $A$ = “Head appears” and event $B$ = “Tail appears.” Are $A$ and $B$ disjoint? Justify your answer.

---

## Question 2: Operations with Events (10 points)

Suppose events $A$ and $B$ satisfy:
$$
P(A)=0.4,\quad P(B)=0.5,\quad \text{and}\quad P(A\cup B)=0.7.
$$
a) Compute $P(A\cap B)$.

b) Compute $P(A^c\cap B^c)$.

---

## Question 3: Conditional Probability & Independence (10 points)

Two fair coins are tossed.

a) List the sample space.

b) Let event $C$ = “Exactly one head appears.” Compute $P(C)$.

c) Are the events “first toss is a head” and “exactly one head appears” independent? Explain your answer.

---

## Question 4: Discrete Random Variables (10 points)

Let $X$ be a binomial random variable with parameters $n=10$ and $p=0.3$.

a) Compute $P(X=3)$.

b) Determine the expected value $E[X]$ and the variance $\mathrm{Var}(X)$.

---

## Question 5: Continuous Random Variables (10 points)

Let $Y$ be uniformly distributed over the interval $[2,8]$.

a) Write the probability density function (PDF) of $Y$.

b) Compute $P(3\le Y\le5)$.

---

## Question 6: Normal Distribution (10 points)

Suppose $X$ is normally distributed with mean $\mu=100$ and standard deviation $\sigma=16$.

a) Calculate the Z-scores for $X=90$ and $X=110$.

b) Determine $P(90\le X\le110)$ using the standard normal distribution.

---

## Question 7: Central Limit Theorem (10 points)

A random sample of 36 observations is drawn from a population with mean $\mu=10$ and variance $\sigma^2=9$.

a) Describe the sampling distribution of the sample mean $\bar{X}$.

b) Compute $P(\bar{X}\le 11)$ using the Central Limit Theorem.

---

## Question 8: Confidence Intervals (10 points)

A random sample of 25 observations from a normally distributed population yields a sample mean $\bar{X}=50$ and sample standard deviation $s=4$.

Construct a 95% confidence interval for the population mean $\mu$.

---

## Question 9: Parameter vs. Statistic (10 points)

Explain the difference between a parameter and a statistic. Provide an example of each from a study involving the average income of a city’s residents.

---

## Question 10: Binomial Distribution & Normal Approximation (10 points)

A company claims that 70% of its products meet quality standards. In a quality control test, 100 products are randomly selected.

a) Identify the appropriate probability distribution for the number of products meeting the standard.

b) Calculate the probability that exactly 75 products meet the quality standard.

c) Using a normal approximation (with continuity correction), find the approximate probability that at least 68 products meet the standard.
