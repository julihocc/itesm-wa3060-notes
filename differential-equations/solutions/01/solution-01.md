### **Solution to Exercise 1: Order and Linearity of Differential Equations**  

For each equation, we determine:  
- **Order:** The highest derivative present.  
- **Linearity:** An equation is **linear** if the dependent variable \( y \) and all its derivatives appear **only in the first power** and are **not multiplied together** or appear inside a function like sine, cosine, or exponentials.  

---

#### **(a) \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 3\frac{dy}{dx} - 2y = 0 \)**
- **Order:** The highest derivative is \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \), so it is **second-order**.  
- **Linearity:** The equation is **linear** because \( y \), \( \frac{dy}{dx} \), and \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \) appear only in the first power and are not inside nonlinear functions.  

✅ **Second-order, linear**  

---

#### **(b) \( xy'' + \sin(x)y' + e^y = 0 \)**  
- **Order:** The highest derivative is \( y'' \), so it is **second-order**.  
- **Linearity:** The equation is **nonlinear** because of the term \( e^y \), which is a nonlinear function of \( y \).  

❌ **Second-order, nonlinear**  

---

#### **(c) \( y' + xy = x^2 \)**  
- **Order:** The highest derivative is \( y' \), so it is **first-order**.  
- **Linearity:** The equation is **linear** because \( y \) and \( y' \) appear only in the first power and are not inside any nonlinear functions.  

✅ **First-order, linear**  

---

#### **(d) \( \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} + \cos(y') = x^2 \)**  
- **Order:** The highest derivative is \( \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} \), so it is **third-order**.  
- **Linearity:** The equation is **nonlinear** because of the term \( \cos(y') \), which is a nonlinear function of \( y' \).  

❌ **Third-order, nonlinear**  

---

### **Final Answers:**
| Equation | Order | Linearity |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 3\frac{dy}{dx} - 2y = 0 \) | **2** | **Linear** |
| \( xy'' + \sin(x)y' + e^y = 0 \) | **2** | **Nonlinear** |
| \( y' + xy = x^2 \) | **1** | **Linear** |
| \( \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} + \cos(y') = x^2 \) | **3** | **Nonlinear** |
